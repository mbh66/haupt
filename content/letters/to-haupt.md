Dear Michael,

You're reading this on a bad day. That's the only reason anyone opens a letter like this, so we can skip the pretense.

I'm writing on the first day of the second half of your life. Remember? You turned sixty on £1 October 2026, and the forty years before it were an apprenticeship. I am publishing this letter because you hold every strategy you write about to one test: it has to keep working once the whole thing is published. The reasons you do this work deserve the same test. If they read true in public today, they will still be valid on the day you need them.

### Where you are standing

You worked on the launch of Iridium and watched a network that covered the whole planet struggle because too few people believed they needed it. You ran Striata's business in Europe and spent three years persuading banks, telephone companies, regulators and the people opening their post that an emailed statement was as good as paper. Both taught you that projects fail because of people far more often than because of technology. That lesson is the whole of your method.

### Why you do this

On 16 September you sat in a conference room in the valley and watched people who had never spoken to each other talk about what they shared. Farmers, the provincial nature agency, the municipality and local businesses were at one table. Nobody had to give anything up for someone else to gain. A government official said it was the first meeting of its kind in sixteen years in the department.

Most of what you have learned since you were nineteen was in that room. Money moves along pathways somebody designed, and every actor at the table acts on what it needs. When a room is run well, every need goes on the table where everyone can see it, and the pathways can be redrawn in the open. You want more value to flow into the living parts of a place than the extractive parts can take out of it. The room is where that begins. The water held in the land is the first thing you are asking the world to pay for, and you want the people who look after it to be the ones paid.

### When the money runs short

You chose to give the method away. Every page on the wikis is free to copy, and the paid work comes from people who read them and want help. On the day the account looks thin, that choice will feel like the mistake. Count what has been picked up and used elsewhere before you change it. Open material is slow to pay, and it pays for a long time.

### When a deal breaks at the money conversation

Every family you worked with on land and legacy before this stopped at the same point: the financial adviser, or pressure from others in the family. It will happen again. The money conversation is where these decisions are made, and an adviser who gives a market answer is doing the job they were hired for. Your work is for the families who have already had that argument. Wait for them, and leave the door open for the ones who walked away.

### When the valley pushes back

A convener takes the friction first. Someone will say you moved too fast, or left them out, or have an interest you did not declare. Some of the time they will be right. When it happened in September, the objection was made in the room, the fix was agreed in writing, and the Forum was stronger for it. Listen for the need underneath the complaint. An actor whose needs are ignored resists quietly and usually wins. One who is heard tends to stay.

### When nothing seems to move

Value can move into a place only as fast as its people are ready to carry it. Capital that arrives ahead of that readiness buys dependency. So the slow years are the work. You keep a register of what should be true, by when, and how anyone can check, and you publish the results whichever way they fall. The horizon is 2038. Read the changelog before you read your mood.

### What you will not take

The well-paid wrong job turns up on hard days. It may be a carbon deal that needs a cover story, or a plan to grow forever on a finite planet, sold back to you in your own vocabulary. You turned these down when you were rested. Turn them down tired.

### Before you say yes to anything

1. Does it move value towards the BioConomy, or prepare a place, a person or an institution to carry that value?
2. Is every actor it names better off, and would it still work once published in full?
3. Is the place or person ready to carry what is being offered?
4. Would you put your name to it on this site?

Then open the changelog and read what is already true.
